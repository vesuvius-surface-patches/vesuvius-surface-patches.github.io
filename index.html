GrowPatch cutoff

Vesuvius Scroll 1 · 16 hand-labeled test regions

Does the refined mesh stay on its sheet?

A small tool that pulls GrowPatch surfaces back onto the papyrus sheet they are following, and a page where you can check, region by region, whether it really does.

How it works, in three steps ↓

01Why this exists

The problem

To read a rolled-up scroll, each papyrus sheet is traced as a surface, and the scan is read along that surface. GrowPatch, the tracer built into VC3D, does the tracing automatically, but it often drifts a few voxels off the sheet, onto voxels between sheets or onto a sheet's edge. A surface that drifts reads gap instead of papyrus, and someone has to fix it by hand.

The idea

Grow a second, independent trace of the same surface prediction in small tiles over the GrowPatch mesh. Where both see one sheet within 8 voxels of each other, move the mesh halfway toward the second trace. Where they disagree, leave it alone. The method never looks at the hand labels.

Why refine GrowPatch instead of replacing it? Our first attempts were standalone tracers, and on held-out test regions they did not reliably beat GrowPatch. So we kept GrowPatch, which already follows a sheet across long distances, and fixed only the small, local drift.

The test

Scroll 1 has small cubes, about 2 mm across, where the papyrus sheets were labeled by hand. We fixed the method's settings on 16 of them, then ran it once on 16 others and measured how much of the right sheet each surface found. Those 16 test regions are the ones on this page.

Why a page, and not just a number

A single percentage hides a lot: where the gain comes from, whether the surface really sits on papyrus, and where things go wrong. Here you can check every test region yourself, slice by slice, including external_10, where the method puts the most surface on a wrong sheet.

    What this does and doesn't show

    The blend finds more of the target sheet in 15 of 16 regions at every cutoff, and never less. But it also puts more surface on wrong sheets, so it fails the safety check we set before running it.

    Looking point by point, almost nothing that was already on the target sheet gets moved off it. The new wrong-sheet area comes from GrowPatch surface that sat on voxels with no sheet label and got pulled onto a neighboring labeled sheet. At cutoffs 1 and 128, most of it is in one region, external_10, where GrowPatch never connects the target sheet to the seed. In short, the blend tightens up whatever sheet GrowPatch is already following. It does not fix a surface that has jumped to the wrong sheet. We also pre-registered seven stricter versions to remove the wrong-sheet rise; none passed on the development regions, so none was run on these.

    The page opens on external_06, which ranks 8th of 16 by area gained at cutoff 128. At cutoffs 1 and 128, the last disc in the tray is external_10.

    Where the numbers come from

    Every score comes from the single locked test run, saved in evidence/tiled/heldout_manual_external.json. To draw this page, tools/explorer_meshes.py rebuilt the meshes from the project's data folder with the locked settings. It stops if any score differs from the saved file, and none did. tools/build_explorer.py then drew the images and checked the counted areas against the same file.
